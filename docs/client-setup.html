Setup guide

B-Roll Librarian

Your footage, watched and described clip by clip, filed into your own Drive folders, and searchable in plain English — "Adam meditating at sunrise" finds the shot, not a folder to dig through. This page gets it running on your own account.

About 30 minutes Most of it done by Claude Your Google account, your bill
Before

What you need in front of you

  • A Google account — the one whose Drive holds the footage.
  • A card, for the Google API billing. Expect a few pounds in total, not a subscription.
  • Claude Code on your computer, with the Claude in Chrome extension connected.
  • Somewhere to run it. Read the next step before you pick.
01

Where it runs

Decide this first, because it changes nothing else but can't be undone cheaply.

Vercel will not work

Vercel runs short-lived functions with no permanent disk. This app is the opposite: a process that stays awake, keeps a database of everything it has watched, and shells out to ffmpeg to read your video. There is no configuration that makes it fit — it needs a server, not a function host.

  • A small VPS best
    Hetzner, DigitalOcean, Linode. £4–6 a month buys more than enough. Full control, nothing to work around.
  • Railway, Render or Fly.io works
    Fine, as long as you attach a persistent volume and let it run as a long-lived service rather than a serverless function.
  • A Mac that stays on free
    A studio machine or spare laptop. Costs nothing, and it is exactly how it was built and tested. The catch: indexing only happens while that machine is awake.
  • Vercel, Netlify, shared web hosting no
    No persistent disk, no always-on process, no ffmpeg.

What the box needs: Python 3.11 or newer, ffmpeg, about 2 GB of memory, and a disk that keeps its contents. Your footage is never stored there — it is read, described, uploaded to your Drive, and deleted.

What this gets you

Claude does the install, the configuration, your folder structure, the first test run and the restart-on-boot setup. It walks you through the Google pages but cannot click them for you — that part is Step 3.

03

The part only you can do

Google blocks automation on these pages, so Claude opens them and reads them out — you click. Do them in this order. Keep the tab open; you will come back to it.

A. Create a project

console.cloud.google.com/projectcreate

Name it something you will recognise, e.g. broll-librarian. Everything else hangs off this project.

B. Turn on the two APIs

console.cloud.google.com/apis/library

Search for Generative Language API and press Enable. Then search for Google Drive API and press Enable. Make sure your new project is selected in the bar at the top — it is the single most common mistake here.

C. Link billing

console.cloud.google.com/billing

Add a billing account if you do not have one, then link it to the project. This is the step that matters most: without it you are on the free tier, which allows 20 requests a day and is refused outright when Google is busy. A library of a few hundred files is impossible on it.

D. Create the API key

aistudio.google.com/apikey

Create an API key in the project you just made, not a new one. Copy it somewhere safe for a minute — you paste it into the app in Step 4, and it is never shown again.

E. Set up Drive access

console.cloud.google.com/auth/overview

Four short screens, in this order:

  1. Get started — app name, your email, audience External, your email again as contact.
  2. Audience → under Test users, add your own Google address.
  3. Data access → Add scopes → paste https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive, tick it, Update, Save.
  4. Clients → Create client → application type Desktop app → Create. Copy the client ID and client secret.

F. Approve the app once

When Claude runs the Drive login, a Google screen appears saying the app is unverified. That is expected — it is your own app, on your own project, used only by you. Click Advanced → Go to (app name) → Allow.

Leave it in Testing

Do not press "Publish app". Publishing sends the app into Google's verification review, because reading your whole Drive is a restricted permission. Staying in Testing is what keeps this a five-minute job instead of a six-week one.

The trade-off: a Testing app's Drive login expires every 7 days. When filing to Drive stops, re-run broll drive login. If your email is on a Google Workspace domain rather than gmail.com, set the audience to Internal at step E1 instead and the expiry goes away.

04

Finish inside the app

Claude will have it open in your browser by now.

  1. Settings → API keys — choose gemini, paste your key, Save. It is written to a private file on the server, never to the database.
  2. Settings → Workspace — set Requests per minute to 0 and Worker concurrency to 4. Those are throttles for unpaid keys.
  3. Upload — drag in a handful of clips and photos. Watch the queue on the right.
  4. Library — check the descriptions and the folders they landed in, then search for something you know is there.

Then go and look in Drive. Your footage is in your own folder tree, renamed so the filename says what the shot is, with videos and photographs kept apart.

One more reason to link billing

On the free tier, Google may use what you send for product improvement, including human review. On a paid key it does not. Your footage stops being training data.
